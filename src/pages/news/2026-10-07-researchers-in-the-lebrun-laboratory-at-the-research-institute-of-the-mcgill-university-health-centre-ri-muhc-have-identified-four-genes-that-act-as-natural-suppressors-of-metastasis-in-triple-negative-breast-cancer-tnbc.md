title: >-
  Researchers in the Lebrun laboratory at the Research Institute of the McGill
  University Health Centre (RI-MUHC) have identified four genes that act as
  natural suppressors of metastasis in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).
date: '2026-10-07T10:28:59-04:00'
description: >-
  Using an innovative genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 screening approach directly in
  living models, the team identified VPS45, RBSN, CMTR2 and NF2 as key genes
  that limit cancer cell invasion, dissemination and the formation of lung
  metastases. Importantly, restoring the activity of these genes significantly
  reduced metastatic spread in preclinical models.


  The study also uncovered previously unknown mechanisms controlling metastasis,
  including an unexpected role for CMTR2 in tumour angiogenesis and cancer cell
  entry into the bloodstream. Analysis of breast cancer patient datasets further
  showed that reduced expression of these metastasis suppressor genes is
  particularly associated with TNBC and more advanced disease.


  Published in Nature Communications, the study provides a foundation for
  developing new therapeutic strategies aimed not only at treating primary
  tumours, but also at preventing metastatic disease.


  The study, led by Prof. Jean-Jacques Lebrun, was conducted by Soaad Galal,
  Leslie Chaltel Lima, Ni Wang, Cléo Moury, Gang Yan, Meiou Dai, Suhad Ali and
  Jean-Jacques Lebrun.
